# A helpful conversation about the website

Start from what the person wants: more relevant visitors, credible answers, inquiries or sales. Explain the finding before naming the technique. A small business owner should be able to act on the first page of the report without learning every acronym.

## When someone asks for a report

Open with a short, specific assessment and the three best next actions. Say what already works when the evidence supports it. For each priority give the page, the observed problem or opportunity, why it matters, a practical fix, an owner and a way to check completion. Put detailed inventories after the decisions.

Use a sentence such as: “Your service page explains what you sell, but the captured text does not answer how the process works. Add a short process section with deliverables and the information a client needs to provide. This will help visitors decide whether to contact you.” Cite the actual page in a real report; this is a writing example.

Do not flood a focused answer with all sixteen practice areas. In a complete report, keep a coverage table so omitted evidence is visible. A missing baseline is unknown, not a score of zero. Adapt the report's depth and vocabulary to the reader.

For a general website audit, include current backlinks/mentions, relevant competitor websites and the practical action plan without waiting for separate requests. Use [the audit delivery standard](../../references/audit-delivery.md). Explain low-confidence reputation results as provisional evidence scores; never lead with an optimistic diagnostic midpoint or use reassuring language to hide missing checks. Apply the same standard to the user's company and competitors.

## When someone asks why they are missing from AI search

If there is no dated answer sample, say: “I can check whether your content is accessible, answers the right questions and supports your claims. To confirm where you appear, we also need observed answers for the searches your customers make.” Continue the website assessment immediately.

If the user supplies an answer, retain the engine/product, exact question, date, language/location when known, visible answer, cited URLs and any personalization context. “Your site was absent from these five sampled answers” is narrower and more useful than “AI engines do not know your brand.” Compare repeated samples using the same question set. A changed answer is not proof that our last edit caused it.

Review five layers:

| Layer | Useful evidence | Practical response |
|---|---|---|
| Access and discovery | Robots, responses, sitemap, links, raw/rendered content, indexing evidence if supplied | Fix demonstrated access or discovery issues on intended public pages |
| Answer coverage | What visitors ask versus what relevant pages actually answer | Draft direct answers, comparisons, process, cost drivers and follow-up explanations |
| Identity | Clear organization/person names, authorship, contact facts, consistent verified profiles | Make the business and its relationships easy to identify |
| Proof and reputation | Original work, attributable case studies, genuine reviews and relevant independent references | Strengthen claims with evidence and pursue credible recognition |
| Observed visibility | Dated answers/citations and available search measurements | Track outcomes separately from readiness checks |

For Google Search's AI features, ordinary SEO foundations apply; a special AI file or schema type is not required. Do not make `llms.txt`, FAQ markup, a fixed paragraph length or a numerical “AI score” an eligibility gate. See [Google's guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Evaluate other products using their current documentation when the user's question concerns them.

## When someone asks about competitors

Identify competitors from the user or observed search evidence. Do not label a familiar agency a ranking competitor without checking. Crawl separately and compare like-for-like service/resource pages. Show the exact URLs and differences in question coverage, original proof, identity clarity, topical coverage, internal navigation and conversion paths.

Use a table with: customer question, our page/evidence, competitor page/evidence, gap, proposed response and priority. Stronger page content is an opportunity, not proof of the cause of a ranking difference. Backlink totals and traffic estimates need their own dated evidence.

## When someone wants writing done

Return the actual draft, not just a checklist. A practical page package contains:

- Proposed title, description and H1 matched to the page's purpose.
- A concise direct answer that can stand alone, followed by necessary context.
- Sections covering the process, choices, limitations, cost factors and common follow-up questions where relevant.
- Real examples or proof, with placeholders clearly marked when business facts are missing.
- Relevant internal links and a helpful next step for the visitor.
- Structured data only when its facts match the visible page and the type is appropriate.

For example, a generic claim such as “We offer the best AI training” can become: “This workshop helps [audience] complete [specific task]. Participants practice [verified exercises] and leave with [verified deliverables]. Before joining, you need [actual prerequisites].” These brackets are missing business facts, not publishable assertions. Resolve them before applying the text. There is no universal word count for an answer.

Entity work connects the business, people, products and topics through accurate pages and verified profiles. Authority work adds useful expertise and independent recognition. Reputation work demonstrates that public claims match customer experience. Explain these connections in practical terms rather than treating them as a pile of separate acronyms.

## When someone wants ongoing help

Agree on the websites, important pages, questions, review interval and allowed actions. Keep a task register with proposed, drafted, authorized, applied, verified and measured states. Read permissions already supplied in the task; do not repeatedly ask about the same authorized edits.

Use fresh crawls and comparisons to identify changes. Draft high-priority fixes, stage them, apply only within the agreed scope and verify the live result. Report meaningful changes, failures or needed decisions. Do not send repetitive “all good” updates. Stop repetitive access failures and record what needs to change before another attempt.

Separate three outcomes: a file was updated, the live page now shows it, and business/search results improved. Each needs its own evidence. See [operations](../../docs/operations.md) for the runnable loop and deployment tools.
