# Competitor discovery

Use [the shared research workflow](../../docs/discovery-and-competitors.md). Establish the client's actual business before searching: home, About, Contact, services, relevant cases, industries and markets. Preserve explicit owner context. Separate offices, served markets, website language and search language. Flag conflicts and unknowns.

Generate queries from supported services, commercial/customer intent, market and language. Use the host's available search tools or native `discover`, with recorded fallback and coverage. Search snippets are leads only. Inspect a broader candidate pool using native page captures before making the final selection.

The assistant writes reviewed natural category/service phrases and customer questions before searching. Preserve useful user phrases; do not stack technical service labels into an invented “money keyword.” Query wording, business-fit assessment and measured search demand are separate. See the shared workflow's `search_queries` format; rewrite unreviewed seeds autonomously.

Reject incompatible services, customers and business models before scoring. Shared words such as AI, agency or design do not make two businesses comparable. Directories, marketplaces, publishers, software products and training providers belong to their actual category; they are not automatically direct agency competitors.

Apply the reviewed profile keys and transparent rubric: services 40, customers 25, markets 15, business model 10, language 5, evidence 5. These are review assumptions, not market metrics. English-language businesses can compete for Pakistan-based customers. An international agency without supported target-market overlap belongs among aspirational benchmarks.

Report only supported competitors, even if that is two or none. For each include exact website and supporting pages, services/markets, why comparable, important differences, confidence and gaps. Keep concise rejection reasons. Identify query/provider/date/actual location-language coverage for search observations; do not call a company top, leading or highest-ranking without appropriate evidence.

Separate direct business competitors, verified query-specific search competitors and aspirational benchmarks. Unverified result URLs remain leads. If search fails, retain the exact failure and continue the website audit with partial competitor coverage. Do not recommend paid subscriptions just because a provider is unavailable.
